# Gaia synthetic photometry handoff

## Goal

Make Gaia DR3 synthetic photometry the primary reference for **every workflow that currently uses APASS anywhere in VaST**. Try a complete local Gaia Synthetic Photometry Catalogue (GSPC) installation first, then query the ESA Gaia Archive. If Gaia cannot supply enough usable matches in the requested band, try APASS DR9 via ESA TAP, APASS DR9 via VizieR, and finally Pan-STARRS1 via VizieR. The two supported Gaia deployments are a live ESA query and a full all-sky local copy. Keep the existing explicit Tycho-2 and astrometry-only modes.

Status on 2026-11-23, evening: APASS and the online Gaia lookup use field catalogs (Design, below). [The fetcher](../lib/photometric_field_catalog.sh) gets the catalogue stars around all good reference stars as one cone, or four for a field wider than 4 degrees in radius, and [the solver](../src/solve_plate_with_UCAC5.c) matches them itself, takes the brightest catalogue star within the search radius as the list queries did, and rejects matches with discrepant positions. APASS comes from ESA, then SkyMapper, then the old VizieR list query, then Pan-STARRS1. Gaia DR3 synthetic photometry comes from ESA, then ARI, and only with `VAST_PHOTOMETRIC_CATALOG=gaia`: Gaia is not the default yet, the local copy does not exist, and the consumers of the output catalogue still take every magnitude for APASS (steps 4 to 7 of Integration and verification). Earlier that day the plan was reviewed against the ESA bulk download, the ARI copy of the Gaia Archive and the VizieR mirrors while the ESA Archive and CDS VizieR were down; ESA came back at 17:34 UTC, and the results measured on it after that are called live ESA results below. CDS and AIP did not answer all day. The live tests of the request shapes led to the field-catalog design, and they found a sign error in the APASS Cousins I transformation, fixed the same day (Known gaps).

## What is implemented

- [The fetcher](../lib/photometric_field_catalog.sh) takes the reference star positions, the search radius and the magnitude range and writes one table of the catalogue stars around them: identifier, position, proper motion, the selection magnitude (APASS V, Gaia G), and magnitude, error and flag in B, V, Rc, Ic, g, r, i, with 0 for a missing value. It covers the positions with one cone, or with four cones fetched in parallel when one would be wider than 4 degrees in radius (Gaia cones 60 arcsec wider, for proper motions). Providers: APASS DR9 from ESA (`external.apassdr9`) and SkyMapper (`ext.apass_dr9`); Gaia DR3 from ESA and ARI (`gaiadr3.gaia_source` joined to `gaiadr3.synthetic_photometry_gspc` with a LEFT OUTER JOIN, so that the stars without synthetic photometry are listed too). The magnitude conditions are written `+ 0 BETWEEN`; there is no ORDER BY. A reply must start with the expected header and have only valid rows; a reply of MAXREC rows counts as cut; HTTP 408, 429, 5xx and no reply are asked once more after 10 s; anything else moves on to the next provider; messages never carry the word ERROR. Tables are cached in `lib/catalogs/field_catalog_cache` and reused when they cover the positions and the magnitude range (1000 MB by default, least recently used removed first). The fetcher honours `VAST_CURL_PROXY`.
- [The solver](../src/solve_plate_with_UCAC5.c), `search_photometric_field_catalog()`: all good reference stars take part, without the old limit of 1000 (the VizieR list query and Pan-STARRS1, which remain as fallbacks, keep it). For each reference star the brightest catalogue star within the search radius and the magnitude range is taken; if it has no usable photometry, the reference star gets none (a fainter neighbour does not stand in): APASS needs B, V, r' and i' (g' may be missing); a Gaia star needs synthetic photometry, no VARIABLE flag and |c_star| <= 0.1. APASS is searched around the UCAC5 positions at the catalogue epoch, as before; Gaia positions are moved to the epoch of the image with their proper motions and searched around the UCAC5 positions at that epoch. Gaia bands are used one by one, the validity flags are not used, Rc and Ic come directly from Gaia, errors are raised to 0.01 mag; APASS Rc and Ic follow Jester et al. (2005). A complete field catalogue with fewer than 4 matches is not asked again through VizieR.
- The positional cut (`reject_photometric_catalog_position_outliers()`) applies to every source, the VizieR list query and Pan-STARRS1 included: offsets of the catalogue position from the measured position, median and 1.4826 MAD per coordinate, sigma at least 1 arcsec, matches beyond 5 sigma rejected, no cut below 20 matches. A source that fails leaves no photometry behind for the next one.
- `VAST_PHOTOMETRIC_CATALOG=gaia` puts Gaia before APASS.
- [The offline test](../util/examples/test_photometric_field_catalog.sh), run from `test_vast.sh`, covers the fetcher and the solver with a `curl` stand-in that answers cone queries from sky stars made for the reference stars of a test image; it checks the Cousins magnitudes against the published Jester et al. (2005) relations (see Verification record).
- Live on the TESS reference frame (35.4 arcsec, V or G from 1.0 to 13.5): APASS from ESA, 4464 positions, 72,456 catalogue stars in 11 s, 2695 matched, 249 rejected by the positional cut (sigma 3.1 and 3.3 arcsec), 2446 used, where the 1000-position query used to give 319; Gaia from ESA, 140,950 stars in 35 s, 3910 matched, 253 rejected, 3657 used. The I-band zero points agree: 25.825 with APASS (robust sigma 0.269 mag) and 25.820 with Gaia (0.143 mag); for the 2211 stars with both, APASS Ic minus Gaia Ic is -0.042 mag (robust sigma 0.16), V -0.008 mag (0.037).

Known gaps (2026-11-23, evening):

- Gaia is opt-in. With it, the consumers still label every magnitude APASS, and nothing records which provider gave which band (step 4).
- CDS TAPVizieR (`I/360/syntphot`, `II/336/apass9`) is not a provider: it was down all day, so its column names and reply format could not be checked. ARI is the second Gaia provider meanwhile; it is much slower (a 0.25-degree field: 50 s against 19 s on ESA) and did not answer a 4-degree cone within 60 s.
- No memory of a failed provider: a provider that accepts the connection and never answers costs up to 3 minutes per cone, twice, for every image. The cache helps only after a success.
- The Gaia validity flags are not used, and the cuts (|c_star| <= 0.1, VARIABLE) are first guesses (Decisions).
- **APASS Cousins I was 0.42 mag too faint (fixed on 2026-11-23).** Jester et al. (2005) give R - I = 1.00 (r - i) + 0.21, so Ic = Rc - (r - i) - 0.21, but `set_apass_photometry()` and the Pan-STARRS1 reader in [the solver](../src/solve_plate_with_UCAC5.c) added the 0.21 (the formula dated from the initial commit of 2019). Against Gaia synthetic photometry for 26,420 stars of the TESS field (APASS V < 12.5, one Gaia star within 2 arcsec), APASS B, V, g', r' and i' agree within 0.02 mag (medians), the derived Rc within 0.044 mag, and the derived Ic was off by +0.367 mag; with the sign of the paper it agrees within 0.053 mag. Every `APASS_I` calibration was affected, the TICA TESS camera among them; the fix moved the expectations of the three TESS sections of `test_vast.sh` by -0.42 mag (the nova of the Nova Vul test, the reference image limiting magnitudes of the calibration failure and zero RA tests, and the six candidates of the zero RA test).
- History of the ESA upload join (replaced by the field catalogs): the client of 2518cd35 ordered by table-qualified names, which the ARI Gaia TAP service rejects (HTTP 400); with unqualified names ESA gave the same values as the VizieR mirror at IDIA, but the join took about 5 s plus 0.12 s per position and ESA ends a synchronous query after about 60 s (HTTP 408), so the 1000 positions of a TESS frame never got through, and every frame with more than about 400 reference stars fell back to VizieR. Tried: a 10 arcsec radius, `vmag + 0`, exchanging POINT and CIRCLE, four parallel batches of 250 positions (30.7 s); a field cone matched locally gave exactly the rows of the upload join in 11.4 s.

## Gaia catalogue

The ESA table is `gaiadr3.synthetic_photometry_gspc`: 218,936,915 sources, all with G < 17.65 and BP/RP spectra. It holds `source_id`, `c_star` and, for every band, `<band>_mag`, `<band>_flux`, `<band>_flux_error` and `<band>_flag`. It has no coordinates. Join `gaiadr3.gaia_source` on `source_id` for the position (epoch 2016.0), proper motion and `phot_variable_flag`. Johnson B and V are `b_jkc` and `v_jkc`, Cousins R and I are `r_jkc` and `i_jkc`, Sloan g, r and i are `g_sdss`, `r_sdss` and `i_sdss`. A live ESA join for a 0.25-degree cone centered at RA 291.769208 and Dec +15.467964 returned 20 validated I-band sample rows for Gaia G between 9 and 14; the same cone on ARI holds 167 such sources. [ESA data model](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3/Gaia_archive/chap_datamodel/sec_dm_performance_verification/ssec_dm_synthetic_photometry_gspc.html) | [GSPC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06215)

- **Each band is null independently.** A magnitude is listed only when flux / flux_error > 30 in that band. In 2070 rows of the bulk download B is missing for 12 percent of the sources, g for 4 percent, V for 0.9 percent, r for 0.2 percent, and R, I and i for none.
- **Uncertainty.** Use `(2.5 / ln(10)) * flux_error / flux`, with positive flux. It never exceeds 0.036 mag and is about 0.001 mag for bright stars. Apply a floor, as [the Seestar script](../util/seestar_photometry.sh) does with 0.01 mag, or a handful of bright stars carries every weighted fit.
- **The flag is a validity range, not a quality mark.** It is 1 when G and BP-RP of the source lie where the standardisation was validated. Measured on 8709 sources with G < 14.5 in a 2-degree cone at RA 100, Dec +30: the V, R and I flags are 0 for every source brighter than G = 10.80 and 1 for 99.9 percent of the fainter ones (the exceptions are redder than BP-RP = 2.6); the B flag needs in addition 0.50 <= BP-RP <= 1.70, which leaves 93 percent of the fainter ones; the g, r and i flags are 0 for every source brighter than G = 14.20 and 1 for all fainter ones. Requiring flag = 1 would remove every comparison star brighter than G = 10.8, the range where Gaia is expected to replace saturated APASS photometry, and nearly every Sloan-band comparison star of a small telescope.
- **Blends.** `c_star` is the corrected BP/RP flux excess. Its absolute value has a median of 0.008 and exceeds 0.09 for 10 percent of the sampled sources; large values mark blended or otherwise disturbed photometry.
- **Depth.** Nothing is fainter than G = 17.65. Deep narrow fields whose unsaturated stars are all fainter keep depending on Pan-STARRS1.
- **Photometric systems.** GSPC g, r, i are standardised to the SDSS system, APASS lists g', r', i'. Cousins R and I come directly from Gaia instead of the Jester et al. (2005) transformation of APASS V, r' and i'. Expect zero-point and colour-term shifts against earlier APASS-based results and measure them.

## Local copy

ESA lists 44 gzipped CSV files totaling 41,976,759,812 bytes, about 42.0 GB or 39.1 GiB, and `_MD5SUM.txt` with their checksums. The files are ECSV: several hundred header lines starting with `#` (389 in the first file), a line of column names, then data with the word `null` for a missing value. They hold `source_id`, `c_star` and the photometry, and no coordinates. The rows are not in sky order: 2070 consecutive rows of the first file fall into 1981 different HEALPix level-8 pixels spread over the whole sky. [ESA bulk listing](https://cdn.gea.esac.esa.int/?prefix=Gaia/gdr3/Performance_verification/synthetic_photometry_gspc/) | [Catalogue statistics](https://gaia.aip.de/metadata/gaiadr3/synthetic_photometry_gspc/)

Coordinates and proper motions come from the `gaia_source` bulk files: 3386 gzipped CSV files, 753,025,661,884 bytes or 753.0 GB, each named after the range of HEALPix level-8 pixels it holds (`GaiaSource_000000-003111.csv.gz`). The level-8 pixel of a source is `source_id >> 43`, so the GSPC rows can first be sorted into the same 3386 ranges and then joined to one `gaia_source` file at a time as it streams through. [The Gaia DR2 installer](../lib/install_local_copy_of_GaiaDR2.sh) streams 588 GB that way: 2 h 8 min on tau with 12 parallel downloads, checksum tests and resume after interruption. The DR3 CSV quotes strings and writes `null`, unlike DR2.

Store the result in the zone format of [the local catalogue tool](../src/catalogs/local_star_catalog.c) with a wider record: identifier, position, proper motion, G, the seven magnitudes with their errors and flags, `c_star` and the variability flag. That is about 50 bytes per source, 11 GB in all. Give the tool a list mode, so that one process answers all positions of an image. Record the release in `catalog_info.txt`, which is written last so an incomplete build is never used. Build the catalogue once and publish the finished files on the mirrors, as for [the nearby Gaia DR3 copy](../lib/install_local_copy_of_GaiaDR3_nearby_stars.sh): other hosts then download 11 GB instead of streaming 795 GB.

## Design (agreed and implemented on 2026-11-23 for APASS and the online Gaia lookup)

Based on the live tests of 2026-11-23 (numbers in Online sources and the Verification record). Two points differ from the proposal: the match keeps the brightest catalogue star within the search radius, as the list queries did (the astrometry is often poor and the catalogues much deeper than the images), and ARI stands in for CDS as the second Gaia provider until CDS can be tested.

- **Fetch the field, not the positions.** Fetch every online catalogue as one cone around the field (center and radius from the reference positions plus the match radius) with the magnitude range of the reference stars, and match locally. Write the magnitude condition index-neutral (`+ 0`, see Online sources). Split the cone into four tiles fetched in parallel only when its radius exceeds about 4 degrees, which in VaST means the TESS frames. Per-position upload joins stay out: 0.08 to 0.12 s per position for APASS and about 0.5 s for Gaia on ESA, and SkyMapper accepts no uploads.
- **Providers, in order.** Gaia: the local copy (not built yet), ESA, then ARI for now; CDS TAPVizieR `I/360/syntphot` (positions in the same table) once it can be tested. APASS DR9: ESA, SkyMapper (identical rows, cones only), then the existing VizieR list query. Pan-STARRS1: VizieR as now. One fetcher writes one normalized table for all of them (position, proper motion and epoch where known, identifier, and magnitude, error and flag per band) with the provider and the time of the fetch.
- **Cache per field.** Keep the normalized tables, so that the reference image and the later per-image calibrations of the same field (forced photometry, monitoring) share one fetch: implemented as a shared cache directory, a table being reused when its cones cover the positions and its magnitude range the requested one.
- **Match in the solver.** For each good reference star take the brightest catalogue star within the search radius (decided with the user: the astrometry of many images is poor, and the catalogues are much deeper than the images), Gaia positions propagated to the image epoch with their proper motions. On the TESS frame, the local match with the rule of the current queries (brightest complete source within 35.4 arcsec) reproduced the ESA upload join exactly, 319 rows of 319. With field cones the 1000-position cap of the photometric lookup is gone; the list queries, which remain as fallbacks, keep it.
- **Reject positional outliers.** For all matched stars compute the offsets catalogue minus measured position in RA cos(Dec) and in Dec, their medians, and sigma = 1.4826 x MAD per axis. Reject a match whose elliptical distance from the median offset exceeds k = 5 sigma, with sigma floored at 1 arcsec, and apply the cut only with at least 20 matches; log the medians, the sigmas and the number rejected. Apply it to every provider, including the VizieR APASS and Pan-STARRS1 readers, which already parse the catalogue positions. On the TESS frame against the measured positions (sigma about 2.4 arcsec), the cut rejected 43 of 323 APASS matches, 39 of them photometric outliers (more than 3 sigma from the zero point), and 23 of 974 Gaia matches, 16 of them photometric outliers. The measured positions are the right reference: against UCAC5 positions the core is 0.2 to 0.3 arcsec for APASS and 0.02 to 0.03 arcsec for Gaia (UCAC5 is reduced with Gaia DR1), too narrow for a sigma-based cut without exact epochs, and they would not catch a wrong UCAC5 match.
- **Requests.** Keep at most four requests of one process in flight; retry HTTP 429, 408 and 5xx once after a pause, then go to the next provider. Production hosts should rely on the local Gaia copy and the per-field cache.

## Decisions to make before coding

- **Validity flags.** Proposed rule: prefer flag = 1, accept flag = 0 where the magnitude range of the image offers too few flagged stars, and log the number of each. Before relying on unflagged bright stars, compare them with Tycho-2 V and with APASS on NMW and TESS fields; the Seestar notes report agreement of the V zero points within 0.04 mag.
- **Bands are filled independently.** `set_apass_photometry()` blanks a star unless B, V, r and i are all present, and both APASS queries require the same. Applied to Gaia, that rule would discard every star without B, 12 percent of the catalogue, whatever band was asked for. The solver does not know the requested band, and its catalogue is cached and reused for other bands. Proposed rule: write every band Gaia has for each star, count usable stars per band, and fill from the fallback providers only the bands that fall short, so each band column comes from one provider. The alternative is to pass the requested band to the solver. Check first that no consumer combines two band columns of one row.
- **Matching and crowding.** The APASS queries take the brightest source within the search radius, which is 35.4 arcsec for the TESS frame, and the reference positions are UCAC5 positions at the UCAC mean epoch of each star (1998 to 2004). For the first 300 TESS positions, 10 of the 77 APASS matches found within 35.4 arcsec lie more than 10 arcsec from the UCAC5 position, which suggests a neighbour was taken; the other 67 are the same stars with the same values at 10 arcsec. The design keeps the brightest star and adds the positional cut, which on the TESS frame rejects about one match in ten. Still to decide: how to treat neighbours inside the measuring aperture of cameras with large pixels (217 of the 974 TESS reference stars matched in Gaia have another Gaia star brighter than G = 13.5 within 35.4 arcsec): add their fluxes (the flux columns and one zero point per band make that exact), or reject crowded comparison stars as the Seestar script does. Gaia resolves pairs that APASS measured as one star, so this changes zero points in crowded fields.
- **One provider per run.** ESA may refuse bursts (it did on 2026-11-09, not on 2026-11-23), so parallel plate solving could hand Gaia to some images and APASS to others of the same light curve, with a zero-point step between them. A provider change inside a run has to be visible and preventable: honor 429 with a spaced retry before falling back, remember a failed service for a few minutes in a shared record, and offer a setting that pins the provider. Production hosts should use the local copy.
- **Second online Gaia source.** ARI is the second Gaia provider for now (the ESA tables, but slow). Add CDS TAPVizieR `I/360/syntphot` between them once it can be tested: it was down on 2026-11-23. AIP did not answer at all that day.
- **Cuts and weights.** Implemented as first guesses: the 0.01 mag error floor, |c_star| <= 0.1 and no `phot_variable_flag = VARIABLE` (constants in [the solver](../src/solve_plate_with_UCAC5.c)). Tune them on a crowded Milky Way field; consider the magnitude-dependent `c_star` limit of Riello et al. (2021).
